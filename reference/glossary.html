Glossary

TermMeaning
Engine HireSoftware vendor selling all-in-one staffing software to agencies (source).
AgencyThe staffing business that buys and brands the software. Engine Hire's direct customer.
ClientA business that requests staff from an agency.
Worker / candidate / contractorThe person who takes shifts or jobs. Used interchangeably in Engine Hire's copy.
Value leverA specific mechanism by which the product improves a customer's outcome (e.g. faster fill). Lesson 2 onward.
Time-to-fillTime from a role or shift being requested to it being filled.
GeofencingVerifying a clock-in happens at the job's location.
ATSApplicant tracking system: tracks candidates through recruitment.
Speed leverSmart matching and real-time job posting shorten time-to-fill.
Reliability leverNotifications and mobile accept/decline reduce missed shifts.
Pay-accuracy leverClock-in data flows to timesheets, payroll and invoices.
Compliance leverTracking licences and certifications keeps workers eligible to work.
Admin-time leverOne tool replaces many, cutting manual coordination.
Fill rate(shifts filled ÷ shifts requested) × 100. Count unfilled requests in the denominator.
No-show rate(booked shifts where the worker didn't appear ÷ booked shifts) × 100.
RICEScore = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort. Impact scale: 3, 2, 1, 0.5, 0.25. Effort in person-months (Intercom).
BaselineThe metric measured before a change, so you can compare after.
CounterfactualWhat would have happened without the product. A causal claim needs one.
ConfounderA third factor (e.g. a pay rise) that moves the outcome alongside the product.
Control groupBranches or workers that did not get the change, used for comparison.
The five levers are this course's own synthesis of Engine Hire's modules, not Engine Hire's wording.
