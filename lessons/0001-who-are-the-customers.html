Who are Engine Hire's customers?

Before you can name the value, you have to know who receives it. "The customer" turns out to mean three different people.

The surprise: it's software for agencies

Engine Hire is not itself the staffing agency. It sells an all-in-one staffing software to staffing agencies, who run it under their own brand. Each agency gets its own app in the app store, which its clients and candidates download.

So there are three groups, and the value differs for each:

GroupWho they areWhat the platform promises them
Agency (the buyer)Runs the staffing businessFaster fills, less admin, one tool instead of many
ClientBusiness needing staffQuick response to last-minute roles, live status in a branded portal
Worker / candidateTakes the shiftsSee shifts, accept in-app, clock in, track hours, get paid from the timesheet

Sources: App page, Role of temp staffing apps, Contract staffing software.

Your focus: the worker. Notice that the worker is not the one paying, yet the agency's results depend on workers using the app. That is why worker value matters commercially, and we'll come back to it.

What a worker actually gets

From the app page: view shifts with time, location and details; accept or decline directly; push notifications; in-app messaging; clock in and out with geofenced location checks; and mutual ratings with clients. The timesheet data then flows to payroll automatically (payroll & billing module).

Caution: figures such as "20% more engagement" and "60% better retention" are Engine Hire's own marketing claims, not independent findings. We treat them as claims to test later.

Check your memory

Who is Engine Hire's direct buyer?

Engine Hire sells to agencies; clients and workers use the agency-branded app.

Which feature most directly reduces pay disputes for a worker?

Hours are recorded at the place of work and passed to payroll automatically, so there is less to argue about. This is our inference from the feature descriptions, not a stated Engine Hire claim.
