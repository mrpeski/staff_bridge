From product modules to value levers

Features are what the product has. A lever is the reason a customer's life gets better. This lesson turns Engine Hire's five modules into five levers, with the worker's view first.

The five modules

Engine Hire's contract staffing page lists scheduling, compliance, payroll & billing, portals, and analytics. Each one pulls a different lever:

Module (stated)Lever (our label)What the worker feels
Smart matching, rosteringSpeedRelevant shifts reach me sooner
Push, SMS and email updates; mobile acceptReliabilityFewer missed or confused shifts
Clock in/out synced to payrollPay accuracyHours counted match hours worked
Licence and certificate tracking, expiry alertsComplianceI'm warned before I become ineligible
Portals, one platform, analyticsAdmin timeLess chasing by phone (mostly an agency benefit)

Sources: modules, worker app. The lever names and the "worker feels" column are our interpretation.

Why levers matter for your goals. To pitch, you lead with a lever. To prioritise product, you ask which lever a feature moves. To measure, you pick one metric per lever, which comes in a later lesson.

How strong is the evidence?

Thin. Engine Hire claims a 75% cut in communication time, and its app page cites 60% better retention. Those numbers come from the vendor and no method is shown. The only independent source I could reach, GetApp, has five reviews; one director praises payments that "auto-reconcile". That supports the pay-accuracy lever slightly, and nothing else. Practice

A worker is alerted that her first-aid certificate expires in 14 days. Which lever is this?

Expiry alerts belong to the compliance module and protect the worker's ability to keep working.

"Our app cuts communication time by 75%." What is this claim?

It appears only on Engine Hire's own marketing page, so treat it as a hypothesis to test.

Which feature would a product manager tie to the reliability lever?

Reminders directly target no-shows and late arrivals. The others serve admin time or visibility.

Retrieve it

From memory, list the five levers and pick the one you think matters most to a worker. Say why.

CheckSpeed, reliability, pay accuracy, compliance, admin time. Many would argue pay accuracy or reliability matters most to workers, but that is a judgement we'll test with evidence later.
