The sixty-second pitch

Now use everything: the three groups, the five levers, the metrics. A good pitch picks one audience and one lever, shows a real feature, and is honest about the evidence.

The five parts

Audience → pain → lever → evidence → test. The same product gets a different opening for each audience, because each group's pain differs (Lesson 1).

AudienceLead withA feature to point to
AgencySpeed, admin timeSmart matching; one platform (modules)
ClientSpeed, reliabilityBranded portal with live status
WorkerPay accuracy, reliabilityIn-app accept, geofenced clock-in (app)

Which lever leads for each audience is our judgement, not Engine Hire's.

A worked worker pitch

"If you pick up shifts, the annoying parts are chasing the agency and arguing about hours. With the agency's Engine Hire app you see open shifts, accept in one tap, clock in at the site, and your logged hours go straight to payroll. Engine Hire says its app improves retention by 60%, but that's their claim; the thing to check is how often timesheets get corrected."

Note the structure: pain, two real features, the vendor number labelled as theirs, and a test.

Practice

Which opener suits a worker audience best?

It names concrete features the worker uses (app page). The others lead with an unsupported number, jargon or an agency-focused claim.

Which sentence reports a vendor number honestly?

Attributing the number keeps the claim honest. The page gives no method for it.

You're pitching an agency owner. Which lead is strongest?

Agencies buy the product, and speed and admin time speak to their revenue and costs. The others are real features but worker-facing.
