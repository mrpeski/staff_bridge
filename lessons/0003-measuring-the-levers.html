Measuring the levers

A lever you can't measure is a slogan. Here is one metric per lever, how to calculate it, and the trap hiding in each.

Two metrics with standard definitions

Fill rate = shifts filled ÷ shifts requested × 100. The catch is the denominator: you must also count requests that went unfilled, which needs a system that records every request, not just confirmed placements (Beeple, GigSmart).

Time-to-fill = time from a client submitting a request to the worker starting. No-show rate = share of booked workers who don't appear (GigSmart KPI glossary).

These sources are staffing-software vendors. The definitions are simple and consistent; I deliberately leave out their benchmark numbers because they are unverified.

Before and after. To show a lever worked, you need a baseline measured before the change. Without it, "75% faster" is just a number. That is why vendor claims in Lesson 2 are only hypotheses.

Practice

An agency received 40 shift requests and filled 34. What is its fill rate?

34 ÷ 40 = 0.85. The 6 unfilled requests stay in the denominator.

50 shifts were booked and 4 workers did not appear. What is the no-show rate?

4 ÷ 50 = 0.08.

An agency's fill rate rises but no-shows rise too. What is the likeliest reading?

Fill rate alone counts bookings, not attendance. Pairing it with no-show rate exposes poor matches. This reasoning follows the warning in this KPI roundup, which is a weak source.
